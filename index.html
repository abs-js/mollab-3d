About do simulador

MOLLAB shows molecules as CPK spheres and two-color bonds. Geometry uses angstrom distances. Motion is a simplified classical model: temperature increases agitation, walls bounce particles, and some reactions run on their own.

Reactions use composition, not the name. They need the right molecules, temperature, and pressure. An H–halogen acid plus Na or K hydroxide gives salt and water. Na or K salts dissolve if water is present. NH3 + H2O make NH4+ and OH− below 100 °C with enough pressure. Fuels of C, H, O, N, and S burn with O2 only above 650 K and 0.15 atm. The equation is balanced from composition. Isolated atoms can fuse when temperature, pressure, valence, and electronegativity fit. Two H atoms make H2 above about 180 K and 0.04 atm.

Pressure is a beaker model: P = nRT/V, with 1 unit = 1 cm and each visible molecule = 0.002 mol. High graphics is a nucleus and electron drawing, not a quantum calculation.
